## Core Idea: Draw Maps, Leave Blanks

Place points of interest on the map but don't fully define them up front. Instead, write 1-3 leading questions per point of interest and leave the answers blank until play reveals them. A good leading question snowballs into more questions ("What forgotten god is the temple dedicated to?" beats "What god is the temple dedicated to?" because it implies a follow-up: *why forgotten?*). Don't pre-plot outcomes ("the seals break in week 5") - phrase the open thread as a question ("what happens when the seals break?") and let play answer it.

## Fronts

A Front organizes an open campaign without pre-writing a plot. There are two levels: an **Adventure Front** is session-scale, tied to one specific problem, resolved or discarded within a handful of sessions; a **Campaign Front** is the slow-burning throughline for the whole game, with bigger and scarier Grim Portents. Dangers can migrate between the two levels in either direction. Typical shape after session 1: one lightly-detailed campaign front plus one or two well-detailed adventure fronts.

**Concrete build steps**: pick the front level → create 2-3 Dangers (hard cap ~3, leave room for blanks) → assign each an Impending Doom → add Grim Portents (1-3 for an adventure front, 3-5 for a campaign front) → write 1-3 Stakes questions → list the general Cast. Fronts are built between sessions, not during play - live tweaks are fine, but the bulk of the writing happens in prep.

**Danger** (within a Front): has an Impulse (what it wants) and an Impending Doom (what happens if unchecked - see danger types below for standard options).

**Portents**: a short list of *visible, discoverable* signs that the Danger is progressing - not secret behind-the-scenes events. Each Portent should be something the players could notice and potentially stop, and should follow logically from the last. "Warlord Grak finds the Key" is not a Portent (happens off-screen); "Warlord Grak's minions begin searching for the Key" is (players can see it and intervene).

**Stakes questions**: a handful of open questions the whole Front revolves around (what's really in the ruins, what will the sea elves do, etc.) - same "leave it blank" logic as worldbuilding above.

**Fronts are flexible, not scripted.** If the players kill the named threat, don't silently swap in a replacement to keep the same plot running (railroading) - instead ask what naturally happens to the Danger now (a lieutenant might inherit the horde with a *different* impulse; a related Danger might split off into its own Front). Adjust Portents when players act on them; that's the system working, not a failure.

## Danger Types (pick a category, then an Impulse + Impending Doom)

| Category | Example Impulses |
| --- | --- |
| Ambitious Organizations | do what's "right" no matter the cost / take by subterfuge / infest from within / establish doctrine / maintain the status quo / absorb those in power |
| Planar Forces | gather worshippers / open the gates of Hell / tear creation down / destroy all order / pass judgment / eliminate perceived disorder |
| Arcane Enemies | seek true immortality / seek magical power / find a worthy wielder / ensnare / fulfill or resent a destiny / hoard and protect a clutch |
| Hordes | grow strong and drive off enemies / breed and consume / defend the complex from outsiders / spread (undead plague) |
| Cursed Places | draw in the weak-willed / spawn evil / grow and tear apart reality / disgorge demons / corrupt or consume the living / be controlled or tamed |

**Impending Dooms**: Tyranny (strong over weak) / Usurpation (rightful order displaced) / Destruction (apocalypse, ruin) / Pestilence (sickness, decay of wellness) / Impoverishment (enslavement) / Rampant Chaos (all order dissolves)

### GM Moves by Danger Type

- **Ambitious Organizations**: attack by stealth or directly; absorb/buy out someone important; influence a powerful institution; establish a new internal rule; claim territory/resources; negotiate a deal; observe a foe in detail
- **Planar Forces**: turn an organization; give prophetic dreams; lay a curse; extract a promise for a boon; attack through intermediaries (rarely, directly); foster rivalries with similar powers; expose someone to an unwanted truth
- **Arcane Enemies**: learn forbidden knowledge; cast a spell across time/space; attack with magic; scry on someone; recruit a follower; tempt with promises; demand a sacrifice
- **Hordes**: assault civilization; embrace internal chaos; change direction suddenly; overwhelm a weaker force; show dominance; abandon an old home; grow by breeding/conquest; appoint a champion; declare war and act on it without hesitation
- **Cursed Places**: vomit a lesser monster; spread to an adjacent place; lure someone in; grow in intensity; leave a lingering effect; hide something; offer power; dampen/amplify magic; confuse truth or direction; corrupt a natural law

## Worked Sample Front (condensed) - "The Great Wyrm Axstalrath"

Setting: Crescent Isle, a small colonized island with a native lizardfolk population under a loose treaty, three human settlements (Port Taramos, Rockbreak, Windward), and a sea elf colony that's just made contact. A dragon appeared three weeks ago, razed the harbor, blew a crater in the island's center, and nested on a volcanic islet - cutting the settlements off from each other and the mainland. A dragon cult has sprung up in response.

**Dangers**: The Dragon (Arcane Enemy - impulse: defend/care for her young; doom: Destruction) / The Dragon Cultists (Ambitious Organization - impulse: worship the dragon as a god; doom: Usurpation) / The Magmin (Horde - impulse: grow strong, drive off enemies; doom: Tyranny)

**Stakes questions**: What's in the caves the dragon's attack uncovered, and why does she want it? What becomes of isolated Rockbreak? What role does the mysterious rune Monolith play? Will the lizardfolk break their truce and side with the dragon? Why have the sea elves revealed themselves now?

Use this as a template for structuring your own Front: setting + inciting incident + 2-3 Dangers each with an Impulse/Doom/Portent-list + open stakes questions + a small named cast (a mayor, a cult leader, a go-between, a rival power) whose motives aren't fully pinned down yet.

A second, more compact worked example ("The Opening of the White Gate" - a planar-incursion front rather than a monster-menaces-a-region front) is in `rulebook-digest/L1-digest.md`, F-042 - worth a look for a different front shape.

## The Inexhaustive List of Questions (worldbuilding tool)

For any subject - a **person**, a **group**, a **belief**, a **place**, a **thing**, or an **event** - ask across six angles: **Who** (identity/relationships), **What** (traits/objectives), **Where** (locations), **When** (timeline/destiny), **Why** (motive/emotion), **How** (methods/tools). Leave the answers blank until play reveals them; use this as a prompt generator when you're stuck on what to ask about something the players just pointed at.
