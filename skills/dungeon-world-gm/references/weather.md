# Weather and Weather Moves

## About Weather

### As Color

Small seasonal or weather details in descriptive color (e.g., crunching dry leaves underfoot, or crystallized breath whitening a beard) connect characters to the setting and underscore that those environmental factors matter.

### As Drama

Bending weather to the narrative can heighten drama by either reinforcing the action (e.g., a lightning strike illuminating a scar at a confrontational moment) or contrasting with the emotion (e.g., emerging from loss into bright, cloudless sunlight).

### As GM Moves

 Weather is a constant tool for complication: use a single soft move for sudden change or a sequence of soft moves to escalate atmosphere and tension (clear skies → clouds → darkness → rain). Rain (and similar conditions like blizzards, sandstorms, or heat) can compromise footing, vision, navigation, provisions, and gear in ways that fit the specific environment; outcomes of related moves such as Hunker Down or Forge Ahead should be tailored accordingly. Treat weather as more than backdrop—link it physically to the landscape and characters to make the world feel alive.

## Severe Weather Moves

 Severe weather moves exist to show how extreme conditions hinder an adventuring party’s ability to travel; create additional ones as needed.

### Hunker Down

When sheltering from the elements, one party member rolls +nothing.
• 10+: weather clears relatively soon.
• 7–9: no improvement soon—Forge Ahead or Make Camp and hope for change by morning.
• 6-: mark XP; GM makes a move.

### Forge Ahead

When pressing on against powerful weather, roll +CON.
• 10+: advance as far as possible before needing rest.
• 7–9: choose one—overtax yourself (become weak, shaky, or sick); gear suffers the worst of the weather (GM describes); or decide to stay put.
• 6-: mark XP; GM makes a move.

If progress is made, the GM says where the party ends up on the map and whether the weather shows signs of easing.
